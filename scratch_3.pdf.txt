A cycle-aware and physics-
informed framework for battery 
remaining useful life prediction
Yixuan Chen1, Yueran Wu2 & Conghui Li1
Accurate prediction of the Remaining Useful Life (RUL) of Li-ion batteries is essential for safe and 
efficient energy systems, but it is difficult because sensor data are multivariate and irregularly sampled. 
Many state-of-the-art deep learning methods are domain-agnostic. For example, T-PATCHGNN 
uses generic patching that cuts charge–discharge cycles into fragments with little physical meaning, 
weakening the input signal and limiting learning of true degradation. We propose Bat-T-GNN, which 
injects domain knowledge at both the input and objective levels. First, Cycle-Aware Patching segments 
the time series by actual charge–discharge cycles, giving the model coherent, physically meaningful 
inputs. Second, a Physics-Informed Consistency Loss (PINN-RUL) regularizes training by requiring 
the final RUL prediction to be consistent with a physically plausible degradation curve learned from 
the data. This method experimented on public benchmarks show that proposed method significantly 
outperforms prior methods, including T-PATCHGNN. Ablation studies confirm that both proposed 
components drive the performance gains, establishing a new state of the art in battery RUL prediction.
Lithium-ion batteries power many modern systems, from electric vehicles to grid storage. Safe and cost-effective 
operation requires accurate prediction of Remaining Useful Life (RUL)1,2. This is difficult because RUL is a time-
series forecasting problem in which battery health declines due to many interacting factors3–6.
The main challenge comes from the properties of real battery data. First, sampling within operating cycles 
is inconsistent and irregular. In practice, a Battery Management System (BMS) does not measure at a fixed rate: 
sampling can be denser during rapid changes and sparser during stable or resting periods 7–9. This violates the 
uniform-grid assumption used by many standard models such as CNNs and Transformers. Moreover, the time 
gap between two measurements is informative, such as response rates, so simple fixes like linear interpolation 
can add artificial points and hide true non-linear behavior. Second, RUL prediction must model complex, 
evolving relationships among multiple variables, including voltage, current, temperature, and internal resistance. 
No single sensor captures battery health; it emerges from their joint behavior. These correlations change as the 
battery ages. For example, internal resistance may have little effect early on, but it increases with degradation 
and then strongly drives voltage drop and heat generation near end-of-life. Thus, a useful model cannot assume 
fixed correlations. It must adapt to stage-dependent interactions to predict critical transitions, including the late 
“knee-point” decline.
Historically, battery prognostics relied on physics-based models that simulate the underlying electrochemical 
processes10–14. While offering deep theoretical insight, many state-of-the-art deep learning methods are not 
well suited to this problem. Transformer models work well for general time-series forecasting, but they assume 
regularly sampled data and struggle with the irregular time gaps common in battery datasets15–17. Pre-processing 
to force regular sampling can introduce errors and hide important details. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) 
can model multiple variables together, but they often assume fixed relationships among sensors 18–20. This is 
unrealistic for aging batteries, where the importance of factors such as internal resistance increases over time. 
Methods based on Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) can handle irregular sampling, but they are 
often too slow for practical use 21,22. The Transformable Patching Graph Neural Network (T-PATCHGNN) is a 
recent model that addresses both irregular data and changing correlations23. However, it has a key limitation for 
battery applications: it splits time series into generic, equal-duration windows that ignore the natural charge–
discharge cycle. This cycle is the basic unit of battery operation. By fragmenting it, the model loses meaningful 
structure and struggles to learn the true degradation process24,25.
