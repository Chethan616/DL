The project in one page

The proposal is technically plausible, but its strongest novelty claim is still an experiment to run, not a result to announce.

ProblemBattery health changes over time

SOH and RUL estimates must remain useful as cycling, temperature, load, and aging change the signal.

InputsObservable battery signals

Voltage, current, temperature, elapsed time, cycle index, and optional BMS-derived state features.

OutputsSOH and RUL together

Capacity health as a percentage and remaining cycles to a declared end-of-life threshold.

ContributionPhysics-preserving updates

A replay-buffer learner that adapts to new battery behavior while retaining old knowledge.

Reported evidence includes a gated multi-task PINN with a simplified Transformer, constant-voltage charging features, model comparisons, training-sample sensitivity, and transfer-learning analysis. The paper uses roughly 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits and reports 20 input features. Its headline values include 0.75% SOH MAPE in the reported NCM case, about 0.01 normalized short-term degradation-path deviation, and 104-cycle RUL MAE in the reported NCA case.
Energy / Gao et al. — electrochemical-mechanism EM-PINN
Reported evidence includes an SEI-layer-growth formulation, separate PDE, boundary-condition, initial-condition, and SOH-physics losses, cross-dataset evaluation on XJTU, MIT, and HUST, and six testing scenarios with different data volumes. The paper’s low reported MAPE is 0.873% in its scenario table. This is a SOH-focused mechanism extension, not evidence for replay-based continual adaptation.
Scientific Reports / Chen et al. — cycle-aware Bat-T-GNN
Reported evidence includes cycle-aware patching by current phase, time-aware convolution, Transformer history modeling, dynamic sensor-correlation graphs, and a PINN-RUL loss. The study performs baseline comparisons, hyperparameter sensitivity, ablations, case studies, adjacency visualization, and robustness tests with Gaussian noise, missing samples, and sensor drift. The reported NASA values include RMSE 0.014, MAE 0.007, and MAPE 0.008.
Complex & Intelligent Systems / Jung et al. — PINN plus contrastive learning
Reported evidence includes NASA B0005/B0006/B0007/B0018 evaluation, CNN/BPINN/Informer/XGBoost-ARIMA baselines, physics-guided augmentation, momentum contrastive learning, ablations, SHAP analysis, parameter count, training time, and CPU inference latency. The paper reports average headline MAE of about 0.0952% and RMSE of about 0.117%; its ablation table shows the full model outperforming versions without contrastive learning, physics, or physics-guided augmentation.
How to read the reported numbers
They are reported values, not a universal leaderboard. The papers use different chemistries, cell counts, feature sets, labels, splits, RUL definitions, and metric scales. The student report should reproduce its own metrics under one declared protocol before making a ranking claim.
Why the review paper matters
The base review is the map for selecting physical constraints and deployment questions. It supports the project’s direction, but it is not the project’s experimental baseline. It does not itself introduce one complete new model or provide one reproducible experiment for this project.
03 / Physics choices

Equations the project can defend

The review paper surveys multiple ways to inject physics. The first implementation should select constraints that the NASA measurements can actually support.

SOH label

Student-selected baselineSOH(t) = 100 × C(t) / C_nominal

Capacity at a cycle becomes the supervised health label. The implementation must compute this from measured capacity and never feed future capacity into the deployed input.

RUL label

Student-selected baselineRUL(t) = N_EOL - N_current

For the primary POC, define N_EOL as the cycle where SOH reaches 70%. State the threshold in every table and figure.

Data and physics loss

Student-selected compositionL_total = L_data + λ_phys L_phys + λ_cons L_cons

L_data covers SOH and RUL regression. L_phys covers degradation-trend and capacity-curve residuals. L_cons keeps the SOH, RUL, and inferred curve logically aligned.

SEI extension

Reported literature directionSEI-PDE residual + boundary loss + initial loss + SOH loss

Gao et al. connect SEI growth to SOH with an EM-PINN. Add this only after verifying the exact equation, variables, and parameter identifiability from the paper and available data.

A strict monotonic penalty can be too rigid because real battery traces show capacity regeneration after rest. Use a soft long-term trend constraint and report the remaining violations instead of hiding them.

05 / Implementation

A staged build that can reach a result

Build the smallest defensible experiment first, then add the heavier electrochemical extension only when the data and residuals justify it.

01NASA recordsAcquire four MAT files.
02Cycle parserOrder, clean, mask, label.
03Cycle encoderSummarize irregular samples.
04TransformerModel history across cycles.
05Physics lossesKeep outputs plausible.
06SOH / RUL headsPredict both health targets.
07Replay updateLearn from new behavior.
08Twin stateTrack version and residuals.
Stage 1 / Data audit

Make the dataset trustworthy

Parse cycles, plot capacity fade, measure cycle lengths, inspect temperature/current ranges, quantify missingness, and freeze the EOL rule.

Stage 2 / Reproducible baseline

Prove the labels and split

Train an empirical trend model, feedforward DNN, LSTM or GRU, and Transformer-only model with leave-one-cell-out evaluation.

Stage 3 / Physics-informed twin

Add joint SOH/RUL learning

Use masked cycle features, a history encoder, two prediction heads, bounded outputs, capacity-curve residuals, and soft degradation consistency.

Stage 4 / Adaptive experiment

Test the proposed contribution

Reveal later cycles in order, detect drift, update through replay, compare against a frozen model and full retraining, then report accuracy, cost, and forgetting.

Initial model set

  • Linear or empirical degradation trend
  • Feedforward DNN
  • LSTM or GRU
  • Transformer-only model
  • PINN without adaptation
  • Proposed adaptive PINN twin

Adaptive update rule

Train on early-life cycles. Reveal the stream in order. Trigger an update when residuals or trajectory behavior cross a declared drift threshold. Keep recent examples and representative older examples in the replay buffer. Fine-tune selected layers with a lower learning rate while retaining the physics losses.

The comparison must include update time, number of updated parameters, post-shift error, and performance on previously learned cells.

Defensible novelty statement: the student contribution is the experimentally verified combination of post-deployment incremental adaptation, replay memory, drift detection, and physics-preserving updates for joint SOH/RUL estimation. “Transformer + PINN + DNN” alone is not enough to establish novelty.

08 / Acceptance checks

What must pass before claiming success

These checks turn “it runs” into a defensible proof of concept.

  • The loader reconstructs the four cells without cycle-order errors and the EOL label matches the declared 70% rule.
  • The split is cell-aware and temporally ordered. No future capacity or future-cycle statistics leak into the input.
  • All baselines train under the same evaluation protocol and report the same primary metrics.
  • The physics model reports bounded SOH, a residual, and degradation-violation rate rather than only prediction error.
  • The adaptive model improves post-shift performance against the frozen model and records its update cost.
  • Replay prevents unacceptable forgetting on previously learned cells.
  • Three random seeds produce mean plus or minus standard deviation, not a single best run.
  • The final table distinguishes reproduction of paper evidence from new evidence generated by the student project.
Small-data risk

Four cells are useful for a POC but weak for broad generalization. Use leave-one-cell-out reporting and avoid inflated random-row splits.

Physics mismatch

Strict monotonic SOH can punish real regeneration. Use a soft long-term constraint and expose violations for discussion.

Digital-twin overclaim

A dashboard or model wrapper does not prove a digital twin. The report must show state updates, versioning, residuals, and adaptation behavior.

Metric mismatch

Paper metrics use different scales and protocols. Use literature numbers as context and make the student protocol the primary comparison.
